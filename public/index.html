Two accounts, one discipline

The work looks different. The standard doesn't.

A commercial Google Ads account and a nonprofit Grant account run on different rules, different budgets and different goals — but both fail for the same reason: nobody is actually watching them closely enough.

For businesses

You're paying for every click. The job is making the account earn that spend back — and proving it, all the way to revenue.

  • Account audits that find where budget is leaking
  • Competitive visibility analysis — where competitors dominate and where you should actually be spending to capture traffic
  • Attribution tied to invoice revenue, not just last-click conversions
  • Monthly reporting tied to revenue, leads or CAC — not impressions

For nonprofits

The $10,000 is already yours. The job is spending it well enough that Google lets you keep it.

  • Eligibility review, application, and Google for Nonprofits setup
  • Campaigns structured to hold the 5% CTR requirement
  • Conversion tracking for donations, sign-ups and volunteer forms
  • Ongoing management so the grant doesn't lapse from neglect

Questions worth asking

Straight answers.

Do you actually manage the Google Ads account, or just consult?

I manage it directly. The same person diagnosing the problem executes the account changes, measurement work and ongoing optimization — this isn't strategy-only consulting.

Do you take a percentage of ad spend?

Mostly no — fees are a clear, scoped amount based on the complexity of the work. Above $12,000/mo in spend, pricing shifts to a small percentage of spend (see Pricing), and that's disclosed upfront, not negotiated after the fact.

Why not just hire a PPC specialist directly?

If all you need is someone to adjust bids and write ads, that's a fair option. PYR fits when the real question is why performance isn't where it should be, and what needs to change around the account to fix it.

Is our nonprofit actually eligible for the Google Ad Grant?

Most registered 501(c)(3)s with a website that clearly explains their mission qualify, with a few exclusions (hospitals and schools have separate Google for Education programs, for instance). Part of the first engagement is confirming eligibility before anything else.

What happens if our account falls out of compliance?

Google can suspend accounts that fall below the required click-through rate or drift from its structural rules. Ongoing management exists specifically to catch that before it happens — recovering a suspended account is possible but harder than preventing it.

Have you actually worked inside a nonprofit, or just managed their ads?

Both. Before this was an agency, I worked for a nonprofit and raised full-time support to serve overseas in cross-cultural contexts — so the constraints nonprofit teams operate under (limited staff time, donor-funded budgets, mission-first decision-making) aren't theoretical to me.
