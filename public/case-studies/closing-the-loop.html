Closing the Loop Between Website Traffic and Store Sales

A large private rental company was generating millions of visits a month, but almost none of that traffic was converting into online transactions. The digital journey and the in-store sales journey lived in separate systems, so marketing spend couldn't be tied to actual rentals.

The problemHigh traffic, near-zero online conversion
The fixBranch-level call tracking linked to marketing source
The resultWebsite visits attributed to signed rental contracts

The pattern

Customers were researching — hitting location pages, product listings and product detail pages, pulling pricing — then abandoning the online funnel.

The website was doing its job as a discovery channel, just not as a checkout. That's a common and easy pattern to misread as "the site isn't converting," when the real story is that the conversion was always meant to happen somewhere else: on the phone, with a branch.

The fix

To connect the two journeys, we added custom phone numbers across the site, mapped to each marketing channel and each branch. Every product detail page got a phone button tied to that branch's number. A database captured the click, the marketing attribution, and the timestamp — then joined it to the backend phone system data: who called, which store, and which campaign drove it.

Finally, the calling party's number was linked to the contract created at the branch, closing the loop from the original ad or organic visit all the way to a signed rental.

Website visit, price research, call to a store, signed rental — all attributed back to the original marketing spend.

The result

PPC and other campaigns could finally be credited for real revenue, not just clicks. Instead of treating millions of monthly visits as a vague brand-awareness number, the company could see exactly which campaigns and branches were converting research into signed contracts — and adjust spend accordingly.

Most of your conversions might not be happening on your site either.

If your traffic looks healthy but your online conversion rate doesn't, the gap is often exactly this: a phone call, a store visit, or a sales rep that nothing is tracking back to marketing.
