How it works

Getting the grant is the easy part.

Staying compliant with it, month after month, is the actual job — and it's the part that gets skipped when nobody owns it.

01

Confirm eligibility & get set up

Verify your organization qualifies, get Google for Nonprofits and Ad Grants access in place, and put a compliant site and conversion setup underneath it before spending a dollar.

02

Build for compliance from day one

Structure ad groups, keywords, sitelinks and geo-targeting the way Google's grant policy requires — avoiding the single-word and overly broad keywords that quietly tank click-through rate.

03

Optimize for mission outcomes

Point the budget at what the organization actually needs — donations, volunteer sign-ups, program registrations, awareness — with conversion tracking that proves it's working.

04

Manage it monthly, permanently

Questions worth asking

Straight answers.

Is our nonprofit actually eligible for the Google Ad Grant?

Most registered 501(c)(3)s with a website that clearly explains their mission qualify, with a few exclusions (hospitals and schools have separate Google for Education programs, for instance). The first step of any engagement is confirming eligibility before anything else — that review is free.

We already have a Grant account. It's suspended, or barely spending. Can you fix that?

Yes, in most cases. Recovering a suspended or underperforming account is harder than building one right from the start, but it's routinely possible — restructuring the account to meet Google's current policy and rebuilding the click-through rate back above the compliance floor.

What does this actually cost?

The $10,000/month in ad spend is free — that's Google's grant, not a fee to me. My fee is a clear, scoped amount based on the setup and ongoing account work, not a percentage that quietly rewards spending more. You'll get an exact number after the free eligibility review, before anything is agreed to.

How long does approval take?

It depends on Google and TechSoup's own verification queue, but it's often a few weeks once your Google for Nonprofits account is validated. I handle the application and keep it moving rather than letting it sit in a queue.

Have you actually worked inside a nonprofit, or just managed their ads?

Both. Before this was an agency, I worked for a nonprofit and raised full-time support to serve overseas in cross-cultural contexts — so the constraints nonprofit teams operate under (limited staff time, donor-funded budgets, mission-first decision-making) aren't theoretical to me.
